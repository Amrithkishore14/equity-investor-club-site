Explained with real-life examples

SIP means investing a fixed amount every month. These examples show what that does over time.

Priya starts with ₹2,000 a month

Priya, 25, puts ₹2,000 a month into an equity mutual fund for 20 years, assuming 12% a year.

Result: She invests ₹4,80,000 in total and could end up with about ₹19,98,296. Most of that growth comes in the later years, which is compounding at work.

Starting 10 years earlier

Same ₹5,000 a month at 12%. One person invests for 30 years, another starts later and invests for 20.

Result: 30 years: ₹18,00,000 invested becomes about ₹1,76,49,569. 20 years: ₹12,00,000 becomes about ₹49,95,740. Ten extra years is worth more than three times as much.

Short goal, 10 years

Arjun saves ₹5,000 a month for 10 years for a house down payment, assuming 12%.

Result: ₹6,00,000 invested could grow to about ₹11,61,695. For shorter goals, keep in mind equity can fall just before you need the money.

Returns used here are assumptions to show how the maths works. Real market returns go up and down and are never guaranteed.
